Game of Thrones - Season 8 Summary

Overview
The final season brings the two overarching conflicts of the series to a close: the Great War against the Army of the Dead, and the Last War for control of the Iron Throne. Alliances are tested, devastating truths are revealed, and the fate of Westeros is finally decided.

Winterfell and the Long Night
Daenerys Targaryen and Jon Snow arrive at Winterfell with their combined armies, but the Northern lords and Sansa Stark are highly suspicious of the foreign Dragon Queen. Tension mounts as preparations are made for the imminent arrival of the Night King. During this time, Samwell Tarly finally reveals the truth to Jon: he is actually Aegon Targaryen, the true heir to the Iron Throne, a revelation that deeply complicates his romance and alliance with Daenerys.

The Army of the Dead arrives, kicking off the brutal "Battle of Winterfell." The living are quickly overwhelmed by the sheer numbers of the dead. The Dothraki are wiped out in the initial charge, the Unsullied are decimated holding the line, and the castle is breached. The Night King resurrects the fallen defenders to fight for him. In the Godswood, Theon Greyjoy redeems himself by defending Bran Stark to his dying breath. Just as the Night King prepares to kill Bran, Arya Stark leaps from the shadows and stabs him with her Valyrian steel dagger. The Night King shatters into ice, and his entire army of White Walkers and wights instantly collapses, ending the Long Night. Jorah Mormont, Melisandre, and Beric Dondarrion also perish in the victory.

The March South and Betrayals
With the dead defeated, Daenerys turns her attention immediately to Cersei Lannister, who has fortified King's Landing with the mercenary Golden Company and scorpion ballistas. During the fleet's journey south, Euron Greyjoy launches a devastating ambush, killing Daenerys's dragon Rhaegal and capturing her closest confidante, Missandei.

Meanwhile, the secret of Jon's true parentage begins to spread, reaching Tyrion and Varys. Varys decides Jon would be a better, more stable ruler than Daenerys and attempts to commit treason. Daenerys discovers this and has Drogon execute Varys with fire. In a desperate attempt to force Cersei's surrender, Tyrion negotiates a parley, but a defiant Cersei has The Mountain behead Missandei in front of Daenerys.

The Bells and the Fall of King's Landing
Fueled by grief, paranoia, and rage, Daenerys attacks King's Landing on the back of her last dragon, Drogon. She easily destroys the Iron Fleet, the Golden Company, and the city's defenses. Recognizing they are beaten, the Lannister forces throw down their swords, and the city rings its bells in a signal of absolute surrender.

However, looking at the Red Keep that her family built and lost, Daenerys snaps. Ignoring the surrender, she flies Drogon over the city, systematically burning streets, buildings, and tens of thousands of innocent civilians alive. In the chaos below, Jon Snow and Tyrion watch in horror. The Hound finally confronts his undead brother, The Mountain, in the crumbling Red Keep; they tackle each other into the flames below, both dying in "Cleganebowl." Jaime Lannister, who rushed back to save Cersei, finds her in the dungeons, but their escape route is blocked by rubble. The castle collapses on top of them, killing the Lannister twins together.

The Iron Throne and the Aftermath
Following the massacre, Daenerys gives a terrifying victory speech to her remaining forces, declaring that she will continue her war to "liberate" the entire world. A devastated Tyrion resigns as Hand and is imprisoned for treason. He convinces a deeply conflicted Jon Snow that Daenerys has become a tyrant and is a threat to everyone, including Jon's family.

Jon approaches Daenerys in the ruined throne room as she admires the Iron Throne. After failing to convince her to show mercy, Jon embraces her and tragically stabs her through the heart. Drogon arrives, discovering his mother dead. In a fit of grief and rage, the dragon breathes fire, melting the Iron Throne down to a puddle of slag before picking up Daenerys's body and flying away across the sea.

Weeks later, the surviving lords and ladies of Westeros form a council to decide the fate of the realm. Tyrion proposes a new system: rulers will no longer be born into power, but chosen by the lords. He nominates Bran Stark—who holds all the memories of the world—to be king. The council agrees, crowning him Bran the Broken. Bran allows the North to secede, and Sansa is crowned Queen in the North. To appease Daenerys's loyalists, Jon Snow is exiled back to the Night's Watch.

The series ends with the Starks heading their separate ways: Sansa ruling an independent North, Arya sailing away to explore what lies west of Westeros, and Jon Snow leading the wildlings beyond the broken Wall into the true North, finally at peace.
