A Knight of the Seven Kingdoms - Season 1 Summary

Overview
Set roughly a century before the events of Game of Thrones (and decades after House of the Dragon), Season 1 adapts George R.R. Martin's first "Tales of Dunk and Egg" novella, "The Hedge Knight." The six-episode season offers a much more grounded, smaller-scale look at Westeros during a time of relative peace under Targaryen rule, focusing on the lives of the smallfolk and wandering knights rather than sweeping politics and dragons.

A Knight and His Squire (Episodes 1 - 2)
The series begins with a young, towering, and somewhat clumsy hedge knight named Ser Duncan the Tall (Dunk) burying his lifelong mentor, Ser Arlan of Pennytree. Rather than give up on knighthood, Dunk takes Arlan's sword, armor, and horses (Sweetfoot, Chestnut, and Thunder) and sets off to make a name for himself at a massive upcoming tourney at Ashford Meadow.

Along the way, Dunk stops at a countryside inn where he encounters a bald, highly intelligent, and cheeky young boy named Egg. Egg begs to become Dunk's squire. Though Dunk initially refuses, Egg secretly follows him to Ashford, sets up his camp, and proves himself indispensable. Dunk begrudgingly allows the boy to stay. At the tourney grounds, Dunk struggles to prove his knighthood to the organizers since he has no high-born pedigree, but his humble honesty eventually wins over some colorful allies, including the boisterous Ser Lyonel Baratheon (the "Laughing Storm") and a beautiful Dornish puppeteer named Tanselle, whom Dunk becomes deeply infatuated with. To afford custom armor, Dunk is forced to sell one of his master's beloved horses to a blacksmith.

The Incident and the Prince (Episodes 3 - 4)
The tourney at Ashford gets underway, bringing several members of the Targaryen royal family to the grounds. Among them are the noble and widely respected Prince Baelor "Breakspear" Targaryen (heir to the Iron Throne), his stern brother Prince Maekar, and Maekar's cruel, unstable son, Prince Aerion "Brightflame."

Tensions explode when Aerion takes offense to Tanselle's puppet show, claiming it is treasonous to depict a dragon dying. Aerion viciously attacks Tanselle, breaking her fingers. Unwilling to stand by, Dunk leaps into the fray and physically beats Prince Aerion—a treasonous crime punishable by death or severe mutilation (losing a hand and a foot). Dunk is immediately arrested.

Just as Aerion's guards prepare to punish Dunk, Egg steps forward and reveals his true identity to the shocked crowd: he is Prince Aegon Targaryen, youngest son of Prince Maekar, who had shaved his head to hide his silver Targaryen hair and sneaked away to experience the real world. Despite Egg's royal status, the humiliated Aerion demands a "Trial by Seven" to settle the matter—an ancient Andal tradition where seven knights fight on each side.

The Trial of Seven and The Morrow (Episodes 5 - 6)
Dunk is faced with the impossible task of finding six knights willing to risk their lives fighting against the royal family for a lowly hedge knight. Through Egg's help, Dunk's own likability, and a growing distaste for Aerion's cruelty, Dunk manages to rally a team. His champions include Ser Lyonel Baratheon, Raymun Fossoway, and, in a shocking twist, Prince Baelor Breakspear himself, who believes Dunk is innocent and wishes to teach his nephew Aerion a lesson in humility.

The Trial by Seven is a brutal, chaotic melee. Dunk manages to use his massive size and Flea Bottom brawling skills to batter Aerion into submission, forcing the cruel prince to yield and withdraw his accusations. Dunk's side wins the trial, but tragedy strikes in the aftermath. Prince Baelor takes off his helmet, revealing he suffered a lethal blow to the head during the fight—accidentally delivered by the mace of his own brother, Prince Maekar. Baelor dies, leaving the realm without its greatest prince and most beloved heir.

In the season finale, a guilt-ridden and grieving Maekar offers Dunk a permanent place in his royal household to officially train Aegon (Egg). Dunk respectfully declines, insisting he must remain a wandering hedge knight to truly learn the ways of the world. Seeing the positive, humbling influence Dunk has on his son, Maekar agrees to let Egg travel the Seven Kingdoms as Dunk's secret squire, provided they keep Egg's true identity hidden. The season ends with Dunk and Egg packing up their camp and riding off together down the dirt roads of Westeros, embarking on their next adventure.
