Game of Thrones - Season 1 Summary

Overview
Set in the fictional continents of Westeros and Essos, Season 1 introduces a world where summers and winters can last for years. The story begins in a time of relative peace, fifteen years after a rebellion led by Robert Baratheon ousted the Targaryen dynasty from the Iron Throne.

Winterfell and King's Landing
The season opens with King Robert Baratheon traveling north to Winterfell to ask his old friend, Lord Eddard "Ned" Stark, to serve as his "Hand of the King" (chief advisor) following the sudden death of the previous Hand, Jon Arryn. Ned reluctantly accepts and travels south to the capital, King's Landing, taking his daughters Sansa and Arya with him.

Before they leave, Ned's young son Bran catches the Queen, Cersei Lannister, having sex with her twin brother, Jaime Lannister. To keep their incestuous secret, Jaime pushes Bran out of a high tower window. Bran survives but is paralyzed and has no memory of the fall.

In King's Landing, Ned investigates Jon Arryn's death and makes a treasonous discovery: King Robert's three children, including the heir Prince Joffrey, are actually the product of incest between Cersei and Jaime Lannister. Before Ned can tell Robert the truth, the King is fatally wounded by a wild boar during a hunting trip (a setup orchestrated by Cersei). As Robert dies, Ned tries to secure the throne for Robert's true brother, Stannis, but Ned is betrayed by Petyr "Littlefinger" Baelish and the City Watch. Joffrey takes the throne, and despite Cersei's counsel to merely exile Ned, the cruel new king orders Ned Stark to be publicly beheaded.

The North and The Riverlands
Following Ned's imprisonment and subsequent execution, his eldest son, Robb Stark, calls his banners and marches south with the Northern army. He aims to rescue his father (and later avenge him) and free his sisters. Robb proves to be a brilliant military tactician, winning several battles against the Lannister forces and successfully capturing Jaime Lannister. By the end of the season, Robb's bannermen declare him the "King in the North," seceding from the rule of the Iron Throne and officially sparking the War of the Five Kings.

The Wall
Ned's bastard son, Jon Snow, travels to the northern border of Westeros to join the Night's Watch, an ancient order of sworn brothers who guard a massive ice Wall. The Wall protects the realms of men from the wildlings and the mythical, undead "White Walkers" that live in the uncharted lands beyond. Jon struggles to fit in at first but eventually makes friends and realizes the true danger gathering in the far North when a dead ranger reanimates as a wight and attacks the Lord Commander.

Across the Narrow Sea (Essos)
In the eastern continent of Essos, the exiled Targaryen siblings, Viserys and Daenerys, plot to retake the Iron Throne. Viserys forces a marriage between the young Daenerys and Khal Drogo, a powerful warlord of the nomadic Dothraki tribe, in exchange for an army. As a wedding gift, Daenerys receives three petrified dragon eggs.

Initially terrified of her new husband and his brutal culture, Daenerys gradually embraces her role as "Khaleesi" (Queen) and falls in love with Drogo. Viserys, growing impatient and abusive, threatens Daenerys; in response, Drogo executes him by pouring molten gold over his head. Later, Drogo sustains a chest wound that becomes infected. In a desperate attempt to save his life, Daenerys trusts a vengeful witch to use blood magic. The magic saves Drogo but leaves him in a catatonic state and costs Daenerys her unborn child.

Heartbroken, Daenerys smothers Drogo to end his suffering. She builds a massive funeral pyre for him, tying the witch to it and placing her three dragon eggs inside. Daenerys walks into the roaring flames. When the fire burns out the next morning, she is found sitting amidst the ashes, completely unhurt, with three newly hatched baby dragons clinging to her—the first dragons seen in the world in over a century.
